Student quickstart | Protocol R8 | Updated October 30, 2026
Prepared by Eldon Marks for the EducationUSA session “Beyond the Prompt,” October 30, 2026.

YOUR EXPERIENCE, YOUR DECISIONS, YOUR WORDS
The coach asks questions and gives focused feedback. You provide the facts, decide what they mean, and write the draft and revision. You can use the same five-step process on paper. AI is optional and must be permitted for your exact task.

THE SAME FIVE STEPS AS THE WORKSHOP

1. CHECK
Before content help on real work, check the current official rules for every applicable institution, application platform and assignment. A teacher's permission does not override admissions rules. Permission for spelling does not automatically allow brainstorming or feedback.

Keep a permission card:
Destination and exact task: _________________________________
Official source and date checked: ___________________________
Relevant policy wording: ___________________________________
The one action I want: _____________________________________
That action is: allowed / prohibited / unclear
Conditions and required disclosure: _________________________

Supply information you already have; the coach should ask only for missing details. If the action's permission is unknown, pause that action and ask the responsible office or instructor. If all AI assistance is prohibited, work independently or with a permitted human adviser. Resolve disclosure requirements before submission. Disclosure does not make prohibited assistance permissible.

The coach is for your own real writing. It does not offer an invented-applicant or demonstration mode. Use your actual experiences and your own draft. Do not supply a made-up profile or prepared teaching essay as a substitute. You can use placeholders and omit private identifiers without inventing your experience.

2. GATHER
Choose one experience and describe what you actually did. Name a strength only if it helps answer your task. Explain your own interest and what you want to learn next. Ordinary experiences count; no award, job or hardship is required.

The coach asks one question at a time and skips answers already supplied. It may ask what you actually did if your experience is vague. It should quote your words back for confirmation, not assign a lesson or strength to you. You may correct notes, skip a question or remain undecided. The four notes, Strength / Evidence / Inspiration / Next study goal, are a flexible inventory, not a required essay structure.

3. DRAFT
Close the chat and write the first draft yourself in your own document. Choose your own words and order. Save the original. Do not ask the coach to turn notes into an essay, hook, outline or conclusion. Your dialect and familiar phrasing are not defects to erase.

Already have your own draft? After the relevant permission check, go directly to the next step. You do not need to repeat the interview.

4. GET FEEDBACK
Only when content feedback is permitted, supply one selected sentence or short passage and one goal. For example:
“I wrote this draft myself. Here is my selected passage: [your words]. Can a reader tell what I actually did? Ask one focused question without rewriting it.”

Expect an exact short quotation, a clear reader uncertainty, one question and an invitation to revise yourself. If the passage already answers your goal, the coach should say so rather than invent a fault. It should not provide replacement wording or a long list of questions.

For “AI-esque” language, examine writing quality: vague claims, inflated impact, interchangeable wording and unsupported conclusions. Compare claims with your actual experience. These features do not prove AI authorship. Do not replace words just to fool a detector.

If your task permits only a mechanical check, request that exact category on your own first draft. Example: “Check spelling only. Show Original / Suggested correction / Reason. Leave my meaning, phrasing and dialect unchanged.” This permission does not permit other help. The correction must stay within the category you requested and the rules permit.

5. REVISE AND VERIFY
Write the revision yourself. You may ask whether it resolves the same feedback goal. The coach should compare your before-and-after wording, not silently rewrite it or start an unrelated critique.

Before submitting, verify:
- Every fact and claim is true, including new details in the revision.
- The wording and decisions are yours; you understand and would use the words.
- The response meets the actual prompt, length and other requirements.
- The assistance follows all applicable rules and is disclosed as required.
- You have kept the original, revision and a brief assistance log.

A useful log records the date, tool, permitted action, accepted or rejected suggestions, and disclosure action. It is a record, not proof of authorship or compliance.

A THREE-MINUTE PAPER EXERCISE
Minute 1: Choose one experience and record what you did.
Minute 2: Note what interested you and one thing you want to learn.
Minute 3: Write two sentences. Identify one claim that needs clearer evidence.
No login or partner is needed. You do not have to share private details.

IF THE COACH DRIFTS
Stop the pass if it invents details, writes for you, repeatedly asks known facts or bypasses a policy check. Do not use an output that crosses those boundaries. Reload the full instructions or continue on paper or with a permitted human adviser. Use minimal excerpts and placeholders; do not assume a consumer chat is confidential. The coach cannot guarantee admissions outcomes, institutional permission or consistent model behavior.

Evaluation details and current limitations are in README.md in the repository.
